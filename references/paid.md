# Paid video creative analysis reference

Load **after `shared.md`** only when `brief.md` declares `Intent: paid`. Do not use this file to infer that a video is paid.

Paid distribution buys the impression; the creative must turn that impression into the next business action. Use the owner's own Ads Manager / CRM results as the performance truth. Never promise a result or substitute universal benchmark numbers for account data.

## Minimum paid context

Only ask for paid facts that materially change the creative and are not already supplied:

1. conversion path — WhatsApp / form / DM / landing page / call / walk-in
2. platform / placement and delivery language
3. funnel stage — cold prospecting / warm / retargeting
4. whether recut, new VO and reshoot are allowed

Do not infer funnel stage from the footage. If it is unknown, mark it unknown rather than silently calling it cold.

## Funnel stage

Judge the same footage differently depending on who receives the paid impression.

| Stage | Creative job |
|---|---|
| **Cold prospecting** | make a stranger recognise relevance, establish the problem/opportunity, introduce proof without demanding prior knowledge |
| **Warm** | deepen a known problem/desire, shorten setup, surface stronger proof and a concrete next step |
| **Retargeting** | remove the final objection, lead with the most decisive proof/offer, minimise repeated education |

Do not turn these into fixed-second formulas. Instead ask whether every beat is earning its place for that audience state.

## Name the sales structure

Identify the dominant persuasion structure before editing:

- Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA
- Before → Process → After
- Customer story → Problem → Change
- Product → Feature → Benefit
- Store/experience → Expertise → Trust
- Offer → Scarcity/constraint → Action

If no single structure can be named, diagnose the creative as an asset montage. If two structures compete, choose one as the spine and demote the other to supporting proof.

## Offer and objection audit

Proof supports a claim. Objection handling removes a reason not to act. Audit both.

### Offer

Check whether the creative communicates any concrete transaction value:

- price/range/entry option when relevant
- warranty/guarantee only when the business can substantiate it
- free inspection/demo/trial/quote or other real risk reversal
- real deadline/scarcity only; never manufacture scarcity
- scope: what is included and who it is for

### Objections

List the top reasons this buyer may still avoid the action, then map footage/copy to each one. Split compound objections. “Will this cause a technical problem?” and “is this permitted?” are different objections and require different evidence.

Never invent legality, compliance, guarantee or performance claims. Narrow the claim or route uncertainty to the enquiry.

## Paid compliance blockers — substantiation first

For paid distribution, treat an unsubstantiated advertising claim as a **blocker** when it could prevent approval or make the ad non-compliant in the target market. This includes absolute/superlative/exclusivity/quantified claims such as `first`, `best`, `most comprehensive`, `only`, `#1`, `over N`, `largest`, or equivalent wording when the owner has not supplied adequate substantiation.

For Malaysia, the Advertising Standards Malaysia code requires ads to avoid misleading or unsubstantiated representations and notes that general superlative claims are difficult to substantiate. TikTok Ads explicitly disallows absolute terms about a product in relation to time, region, or brand, and lists exaggerated/overstated claims among common reasons ads fail review. Therefore, when the paid platform is unknown, **block paid delivery until the claim is substantiated or narrowed** rather than promising that every platform will reject it.

Do not require the proof to appear inside the video itself when credible owner-supplied or authoritative substantiation exists outside the edit; the analysis must simply be able to verify the claim before approving paid delivery. If no substantiation is available, remove/narrow the claim or replace it with directly provable wording.

## CTA and conversion path

Paid creative needs a specific, low-friction next action.

Check:

- one primary action, not several competing ones
- the viewer knows what they get after acting
- the CTA tells them what to send/do when that reduces composition effort
- contact detail / handle / location / button-critical text remains readable and inside the intended placement safe zone
- the landing/chat/form experience matches the promise in the video

A generic “contact us” is weaker than an action with a defined input and outcome.

## Cost-per-result diagnosis

Ask for the owner's own data when available; do not invent benchmark bands.

Useful fields:

- early-view / hold metrics available in that ad account
- completion / average watch time where available
- outbound CTR or equivalent click/action rate
- **cost per result** for the actual conversion event
- CPM, frequency, spend and days live
- lead quality / close outcome when the business can connect it

Interpret patterns as hypotheses to test:

| Pattern | Creative question |
|---|---|
| weak early viewing | is frame 0 unclear, static or irrelevant; is the first line slow? |
| strong start then sharp drop | did the hook overpromise; did the body shift from giving/showing to explanation; is proof late? |
| watchable but weak action | is the offer/CTA unclear, low-value, unreadable or too late structurally? |
| clicks but expensive final result | does the landing surface, offer or qualification step break the promise? |
| enquiries but weak closes | did the creative qualify price/scope/trust poorly? |
| performance decays while frequency rises | test creative fatigue before rewriting a structurally sound message |

Views/reach alone do not prove a paid creative is a winner.

## Paid judgment axes after the shared three

The first three qualitative axes are defined in `shared.md`:

1. Frame 0 + Hook
2. Retention
3. Payoff + Proof

Then add paid-specific judgments:

4. **Offer + objection handling** — is there a real offer/risk reduction and does the creative address the key reason not to act?
5. **CTA + conversion path** — is the next step singular, specific, readable and matched to the destination?
6. **Message clarity** — one hero message; price/location/scope legible when they matter?
7. **Ownership / differentiation** — could a competitor swap in its logo without changing the creative?

Use short qualitative judgments with evidence. **Do not calculate a cross-axis average.** A blocker from `shared.md` stays above the whole evaluation and is never diluted by stronger axes.

Report separately:

- recut difficulty: Low / Medium / High
- re-render requirement: None / Recommended / Required
- reshoot requirement: None / Recommended / Required
- execution risk: Low / Medium / High
- material usability: picture, text defects, audio/VO, proof availability

## Recut vs reshoot

Default to recut when the necessary function already exists in the footage. Reshoot only what editing cannot create.

**Recut when:** proof exists but is buried; order is wrong; copy layers can be replaced; the usable picture/audio already supports the claim.

**Reshoot when:** decisive proof does not exist; the only needed picture is below delivery quality; burned-in false text cannot be removed; before/after was captured incompatibly; a required claim has no evidence.

Every required reshoot item needs a fallback using existing material where possible, plus the consequence if the fallback is used.

### Reshoot list format

Rank `Required / Recommended`. For each item state:

- exact content in frame
- what must be readable/visible
- capture conditions (angle, exposure, distance, ambient light when proof comparison requires them)
- usable duration
- where it lands in the rebuilt cut
- function: hook / proof / trust / offer / objection / CTA
- fallback existing shot, if any

For replacement VO, preserve speaker/mic/room when matching existing audio.

## EDL rebuild

When a rebuild is requested, deliver an executable edit decision list rather than abstract advice.

Columns:

`Time | Duration | Source + timecode | On-screen text | VO | Edit function`

Rules:

- every row has one distinct function
- every visual names a real source timecode or `RESHOOT-N`
- every VO line is tagged existing + source timecode or new recording
- every subtitle/claim must be supported by the picture/audio in that row
- do not use two rows to show the same evidence under different labels
- one row, one message; split compound objections into separate jobs
- total the runtime and verify the arithmetic

For multi-source rebuilds, make a function map and gap matrix first. Choose the source with the best structure as the skeleton and the source with the best picture as the body; they need not be the same file.

## Testable paid variants — required

Paid analysis should not end with one supposedly perfect cut. Deliver a small family of controlled variants the owner can actually test. Keep one meaningful variable different while holding the rest of the timeline constant.

Always identify which of these are testable for the current footage:

- **hook / frame 0** — visual opening or first spoken/on-screen line
- **thumbnail / cover frame** — where the placement exposes a selectable preview/cover
- **proof order / payoff placement**
- **offer framing**
- **CTA copy / requested action**

Give at least two useful variants when the material supports them, name the single variable changed, and state what evidence/result would change the next creative decision. Do not deliver only one recut and call the testing job finished.

## Placement and technical facts — official sources only

These are specifications, not ranking weights or performance promises.

### Meta / Instagram Reels ads

- Full-screen vertical is the intended Reels-ad format; 9:16 is the preferred mobile creative shape.
- Meta recommends music/sound, and its Reels-ad materials emphasise keeping key creative elements inside the safe zone.
- Instagram Reels ad creative can currently be up to 15 minutes in Ads Manager. Do not treat that maximum as a recommended creative length.
- Use original or royalty-free audio as required by the ad workflow; do not assume the organic licensed-music library is available for ads.

Official sources, read 2026-08-17:
- https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads
- https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/546362593027755
- https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/864398265379023

### TikTok Ads

- TikTok's current performance-ad guidance recommends sound/music, vertical 9:16, at least 720p, and keeping content within the UI safe zone.
- TikTok advertising policy currently requires supported ad formats to contain audio; non-Spark/reservation in-feed specifications commonly use a 5–60 second acceptance window. Treat the applicable placement's live spec as authoritative rather than turning this into a universal organic duration rule.
- Safe zone depends on format, caption length and interactive add-ons; use the platform preview/templates rather than a made-up “bottom 20%” number.
- AI-generated, synthetic or significantly manipulated ad media requires the applicable TikTok disclosure/label.

## Paid output

Follow the report structure in `SKILL.md`. Within it, make sure paid analysis includes the paid-specific judgments, conversion-path diagnosis, and **testable variants**. Do not add an overall numeric score.

A multi-source rebuild can additionally include function map, gap matrix, material allocation, re-render/reshoot list and controlled test plan. Do not pad the report, but do not omit executable rebuild artefacts when the owner asked for them.
